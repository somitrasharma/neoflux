**NeoFlux: integrating non-canonical neoantigens, antigen processing, and immune escape in a unified pipeline**

Somitra Sharma, [Your Institution]

**Background** Standard neoantigen pipelines target single-nucleotide variants (SNVs) from exome sequencing, ignoring non-canonical sources (intergenic, intronic, non-coding) that comprise >50% of actionable tumor antigens in melanoma/NSCLC [1] and the predominant class in colorectal/ovarian cancers [2]. No existing open framework systematically integrates these non-canonical sources with antigen processing, HLA loss of heterozygosity (LOH) detection, and machine learning prioritization.

**Methods** NeoFlux, a Nextflow-orchestrated pipeline with a Python CLI, generates neoantigen candidates from six variant classes (SNV, indel, frameshift, fusion, splice, nCORF). Candidates are evaluated via MHCflurry binding [3], proteasomal cleavage/TAP transport PSSMs, VAF clonality, HLA LOH detection, and an XGBoost/LightGBM ensemble trained on 140,676 IEDB records. Non-canonical candidates lack wildtype comparators, receiving self_similarity = 0.0 (maximal foreignness) as a model feature; missing clonality/expression features are handled natively by the gradient-boosted ensemble. This foreignness signal is motivated by evidence that indels generate more epitopes and are up to 9-fold enriched for mutant-specific binding than SNVs [4]. On held-out IEDB test data (n=28,136), the ensemble (AUROC 0.76) significantly outperformed a binding-affinity-only baseline (AUROC 0.54, DeLong p<0.001).

**Results** In a 5-patient melanoma cohort harboring driver mutations (BRAF V600E, NRAS Q61R, TP53 R248W, EML4-ALK fusion, MET exon 14 skipping, LINE-1 retroelement), NeoFlux identified 1,745 candidates (326 non-canonical: 53 fusion, 198 splice, 75 nCORF). Peptides were constructed from the canonical reference proteome (UniProt), so variant coordinates yield real sequences (e.g. EGFR G719S VLSSGAFGT, TP53 R248W GGMNWRP, EML4-ALK junction VEPTPGKGPYRRKHQ). Scored by the IEDB-trained ensemble, three of the top four cohort candidates were non-canonical: a MET exon-14 splice peptide (HLA-C*04:01, 0.72), an EML4-ALK fusion junction (HLA-C*03:04, 0.69), and a LINE-1 nCORF (HLA-C*03:04, 0.69), alongside the highest-scoring candidate, an EGFR G719S SNV (HLA-A*02:01, 0.73). Antigen processing supported presentation (cleavage mean 0.53, TAP mean 0.53). HLA LOH in one patient (20% prevalence) applied a 0.5× immune-escape penalty, halving the affected HLA-A*02:01 splice candidate from 0.60 to 0.30. Overall, 226 candidates exceeded the priority threshold (>0.5).

**Conclusions** NeoFlux provides a unified, containerized, open-source pipeline for neoantigen discovery across six variant classes. It integrates antigen processing and immune escape, with the machine-learning ensemble achieving significantly superior accuracy over binding affinity alone on held-out IEDB data (AUROC 0.76 vs. 0.54). The cohort demonstrated end-to-end candidate construction and scoring across all six variant classes and HLA-LOH immune-escape penalties, with non-canonical antigens (splice, fusion, nCORF) occupying three of the top four cohort predictions under the ensemble even without an explicit foreignness prior. Future directions include clinical cohort validation, MHC class II integration, and prospective experimental validation.
